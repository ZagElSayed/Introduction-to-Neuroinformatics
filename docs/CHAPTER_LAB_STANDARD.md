# Companion laboratory standard — all 28 chapters

Every chapter in the textbook should end with a **Hands-On and Computational Exploration** section, cross-linked to `chapters/chXX-.../` in this repository. The activity must derive from that chapter's actual scientific learning objectives, not from a generic AI example.

Each published laboratory pack should include: (1) clinical/scientific motivation and measurable learning goals; (2) prerequisites, installation and exact commands; (3) a verifiable public data source with version, citation, DOI or stable URL, terms of use, and acquisition notes; (4) a runnable Python script or Jupyter notebook; (5) stepwise interpretation questions with no unwarranted medical claims; (6) required figures, metadata, and reproducibility outputs; (7) an optional graduate extension; and (8) tests or explicit verification steps.

Never commit restricted, confidential, identifiable, or otherwise improperly licensed recordings. Prefer reproducible download instructions over copying large datasets into Git. Small redistribution-permitted examples may be included only after their licensing and provenance are checked. Keep outputs and local raw files untracked.

Pedagogy should build from elementary signal exploration (early chapters) through reproducible pipelines, classical/deep learning, interpretable AI, BCI, and clinical translation (later chapters). An AI model must not be described as validated for patient care based only on held-out window accuracy. Evaluate participants and sites appropriately, prevent leakage, report uncertainty, and keep data provenance.

Book manuscripts and illustrations live in [the book repository](https://github.com/ZagElSayed/Introduction-to-Neuroinformatics-Book). Runnable laboratory materials live here. The two repositories should be updated together and every book lab should state its matching companion directory and data source.
