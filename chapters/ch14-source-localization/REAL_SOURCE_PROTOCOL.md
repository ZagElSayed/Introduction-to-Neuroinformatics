# Real EEG/MEG source-modeling protocol

Use a licensed public sample or instructor-approved recording with compatible anatomy. This protocol is an advanced exercise, not an implemented part of the dimensionless toy script. Record installed software versions and use their matching documentation.

1. **Define the claim and inputs.** Specify evoked response, signed source time series, source power or another endpoint; time/frequency window; source space; orientation; anatomical ROI; and independent statistical unit. Preserve the reviewed preprocessing derivative and its exclusions. A source map is a candidate until scientific QC and review are complete.
2. **Audit geometry and identity.** Check subject/session, channel names and order, channel types, digitization, fiducial definitions, coordinate frames, lengths and transform direction. Distinguish MRI voxel indices, MRI surface coordinates, scanner RAS and head/device coordinates. Record meters versus millimeters explicitly. A 10-mm translation is 0.010 m. Rigid sensor-to-MRI coregistration and nonlinear group morphing are different operations.
3. **Inspect coregistration and anatomy.** Save several views of sensors, fiducials, scalp points and surfaces. Summarize fit residuals and inspect local failures, left/right swaps, implausible electrode positions and segmentation. A low mean distance is not proof of correct alignment. Record individual versus template anatomy and justify model fidelity, tissue conductivities and source coverage.
4. **Keep measurement operators consistent.** Reference, channel selection/order, bad-channel handling and projections must agree across data, gain and covariance. For a linear sensor operation A, the matching quantities are Ay, AG and A C_noise A-transpose. Estimate effective rank; do not invert a removed direction. Follow the installed MNE requirements for EEG average-reference projectors. MEG empty-room noise needs compatible channel selection and processing; it does not model every source of participant noise.
5. **Build and identify the forward model.** Include anatomy/segmentation, source basis and normals, head model/conductivities, sensor geometry, transforms and solver configuration in its dependency record. Changing a transform requires a new forward solution and dependent inverse estimates; changing only inverse regularization need not rebuild the physical forward model. Record the source-space vertex/grid identity rather than only its nominal spacing.
6. **Specify inverse and covariance.** State dipole, distributed or beamformer assumptions; noise interval/estimator; retained rank; source orientation/depth weights; normalization; regularization convention; and output units. For evoked averages, account for trial-count scaling consistently. Beamformer data covariance represents signal-containing data and is distinct from noise covariance; DICS uses a cross-spectral matrix, not a vector of channel PSDs. For contrasts, justify a common filter/normalization when the comparison requires comparable spatial transfer functions; avoid fitting a filter to a desired condition effect.
7. **Assess evidence and sensitivity.** Inspect sensor predictions and residual topographies in the correct reference/whitened subspace, not only a fit percentage. Compare justified priors and regularization, and inspect point-spread/cross-talk behavior where applicable. Model-based resolution does not include unknown head-model error automatically. In a separate copied run, apply a declared 5-mm rigid translation to a copy of the head-to-MRI transform; state its frame and composition side, recompute the forward/inverse and compare results. This is a controlled stress test, not an estimate of actual registration uncertainty. Preserve the original run.
8. **Summarize and compare carefully.** Retain signed orientation conventions when extracting cortical ROI time series; opposing normals can cancel. A vector norm is nonlinear and changes what a spectrum measures. Do not equate nAm with noise-normalized scores or interpret a score as a probability. Record morph/atlas and ROI aggregation rules, treat participants as the independent unit for cohort inference, and account for multiplicity and differences in anatomy quality. Keep spatial leakage and connectivity inference for Chapter 15.
9. **Export a reviewable handoff.** Save source arrays and named coordinates, physical/normalized units, source/model/operator identities, hashes, software/configuration, covariance/rank, QC and sensitivity results, decision/reviewer, and missing/failed cases. Pending, excluded and accepted derivatives must remain distinct. Do not promote the brightest vertex to an exact anatomical or clinical conclusion.
